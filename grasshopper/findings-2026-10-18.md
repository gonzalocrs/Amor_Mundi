# Findings for the report, 18 Oct 2026

Model: grid A, 6 columns per side, middle rings at +4.80 and +6.58, base ring 300 x 300 cast to 20 buttresses
(corner buttresses removed), footing 600 x 300 x 2200. Japanese allowable stress: LONG = 1.0 G, SHORT = 1.0 G + 1.0 W
(typhoon qp 2.6 kN/m2, assumed).

## 1. The printed shell is not structural

- Until now the script modelled the two 30 mm printed skins as a structural sandwich shell. That shell carried the
  roof's push to the hips in its own plane, so the ribs and rings looked almost idle (column My about 1 kNm, base ring
  bending about 6 kNm, middle rings about 6 kN).
- A 30 mm printed skin cannot be relied on: tension across print layers is weak and unreliable, the bond to the cast
  ribs is not guaranteed, and the pieces are printed separately and joined.
- Decision: the shell is a casing that only adds weight. The script now does this by default
  (`shell_structural` False: shell stiffness / 1000, weight kept). The ribs, rings and buttresses carry everything.

## 2. With a casing-only shell, the ribs govern

Column bending My, worst case, 150 x 250 rib:

| Case | Demand | Capacity (150 x 250, d 210) | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| LONG (weight) | 11.1 kNm | 11.7 kNm (concrete 10 MPa, steel 215 MPa) | 94% |
| SHORT (weight + typhoon) | 23.6 kNm | 26.0 kNm (concrete 20 MPa, steel 345 MPa) | 91% |

- 100 x 200 ribs fail (capacity about 10 kNm short-term).
- The typhoon governs on the windward face only, but the wind can come from any side, so all faces need the same ribs.
- Weight alone is just as critical because the long-term allowable stresses are half the short-term ones.

## 3. The peak is at the rib foot, not in the span

- Every peak sits at the first segment of each column, where it is cast into the base ring (a joint moment).
  Along the span the moments are only 1 to 4 kNm.
- Adding a third, low ring (+3.50) did not change the peaks (11.1 / 23.6 kNm). More rings do not help.
- A bigger rib all the way up attracts more moment (22.9 -> 24.1 kNm going from 100 x 200 to 150 x 250).

## 4. Fix: tapered ribs, deeper at the eave

- Columns and hips taper continuously from 350 deep at the eave to 250 at the top, 150 wide. The rib protrudes into
  the room (the print was already planned with the rib protruding inside).
- Depth is more efficient than width: capacity grows with depth squared (M = C b d2).

| Foot section | LONG capacity | SHORT capacity | Use LONG / SHORT |
|---|---|---|---|
| 150 x 350 | 25.5 kNm | 56.6 kNm | 44% / 42% |

- Hand formula: C = 1/2 fc k j; long-term k 0.41, j 0.86, C 1.77 MPa; short-term k 0.465, j 0.845, C 3.93 MPa;
  d = depth - 40 mm cover.
- Final: columns and hips taper 350 at the eave -> 150 at the top (`rib_d_eave` 350, `rib_d_top` 150, output `rib_h`).

## 5. The middle rings bend sideways: 250 x 250

- With the casing-only shell the middle rings (+4.80, +6.58) bend sideways in the face plane (Mz). At 150 wide
  Mz 9.2 kNm LONG against a 5.4 kNm capacity (172%): fails.
- Fix: middle rings 250 x 250 (`hring_w` 250, output `rib_b` -> both widths). A wider ring is stiffer, so it attracts
  more Mz (9.2 -> 14.1 kNm LONG) but its capacity grows faster.

## 6. Final run (LONG and SHORT), all elements

Capacity M = C b d2, d = h - 40. Use = demand / capacity; rings: My/cap + Mz/cap (peaks added, conservative).

| Element | Section | LONG peak | Use LONG | SHORT peak | Use SHORT |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Columns (foot) | 150 x 350 | My 11.2 kNm | 44% | My 26.4 kNm | 47% |
| Hips (foot) | 150 x 350 | My 6.4 kNm | 25% | My 8.6 kNm | 15% |
| Middle rings | 250 x 250 | My 4.8 + Mz 14.1 | 97% | My 9.0 + Mz 14.7 | 55% |
| Base ring | 300 x 300 | My 9.5, N +21.5 tension | 26% | My 9.8, N +26.0 | 12% |
| Buttress caps | 300 x 300 | My 5.1 | 14% | My 11.9 | 15% |
| Crown | as modelled | My 3.9 | small | My 5.0 | small |

- Middle-ring capacity 19.5 kNm LONG / 43.3 SHORT per axis; the 97% adds two peaks that are not at the same point.
  Biaxial check = linear interaction My/My,cap + Mz/Mz,cap <= 1 (Bresler 1960; EC2 5.8.9 exponent a = 1 for
  N < 0.1 squash load). Never the vector sum (unsafe for a square section). LONG governs the rings: wind barely
  changes Mz, while SHORT allowables are about twice LONG.
- Base ring tension 26 kN on 4-D16 (804 mm2 x 215 MPa = 173 kN): OK.
- Column compression 45 kN = 0.9 MPa: negligible.
- Total vertical reaction LONG 1619 kN = model weight (67.5 m3): equilibrium checks. SHORT 1529 kN (wind lifts
  90 kN net), total horizontal 116.5 kN.
- Footing: the tapered ribs make the roof 5.7% heavier (717.6 kN), so roof share and push scale x1.057 (wind part
  included, conservative): H 22.1 kN, V 66.2 kN. Tipping SF 1.61, soil 123 kPa OK; sliding x1.50, AT THE LIMIT.
  Options: footing and strip 700 wide (about 1.55), a 200 mm shear key, or confirm friction above 0.5.

## 7. Karamba display lesson

- Stress/Strength Ratio and the Utilization colours compare against plain concrete (no rebar), so tension reads
  hundreds of percent and means "needs steel here", not failure. Use the section forces (My, Nx with Numbers) and
  compare by hand with the reinforced capacity.
