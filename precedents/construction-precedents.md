# Construction precedents: printed pieces + cast ribs, minimal scaffolding

These precedents back the construction strategy for the 5-supports-per-side branching roof (iteration #16). The strategy is:

- printed triangular pieces carry half of every rib as a channel;
- rebar and concrete go into the channels, so the cast ribs carry the load;
- thin pieces work as permanent formwork, with insulation inside and a cast topping;
- pieces are thicker at the base and thinner at the top;
- the dome is built ring by ring with as little scaffolding as possible.

Compiled 2026-11-02. Each entry gives what was done, what we take from it, and the source. Facts are as stated by the sources linked. Where the sources were thin or disagreed, the entry says so.

---

## 1. Prefabricated pieces + cast ribs (our core system)

### Pier Luigi Nervi: Turin Exhibition Hall B (1947–54)
- **What:** prefabricated ferrocement "wave" elements, about 4 cm thick, set on tubular reinforcement. Reinforced concrete ribs were cast along the ridges and recesses of the waves, making the whole roof integral. A special scaffolding was used for installation.
- **Takeaway:** this is the closest documented match to our system. Thin precast pieces form the rib, the rib is cast in situ with rebar, and pieces and ribs become one structure.
- **Sources:** [Laboratorio Nervi, Hall B](https://www.laboratorionervi.polimi.it/en/?p=1500), [Domus archive](https://domusweb.it/en/from-the-archive/2011/04/28/turin-exhibition-br--palace.html)

### Pier Luigi Nervi: Palazzetto dello Sport, Rome (1956–57, with A. Vitellozzi)
- **What:**
  - A diamond-ribbed dome. Instead of wooden rib formwork, the roof was broken into pieces fabricated on site and assembled on a **light, discontinuous scaffold**.
  - The dome was erected in about **40 days**.
  - The scaffolding tube-couplers were treated as part of the "Sistema Nervi".
- **Takeaway:**
  - prefabrication plus a *light, discontinuous* scaffold (not full falsework);
  - repetition of a few piece types;
  - very fast erection.
- **Not confirmed by the sources found:** exactly how the joints between pieces were filled. I said earlier that the ribs were cast between the pieces; that is documented for Turin Hall B (above) but **not confirmed for the Palazzetto**. Check Gargiani & Bologna, *The Rhetoric of Pier Luigi Nervi* (EPFL Press, 2016) before citing it.
- **Sources:** [Laboratorio Nervi, Sports Hall Rome](https://www.laboratorionervi.polimi.it/en/?p=1504), [Pier Luigi Nervi system](https://www.irenebrination.com/2021/08/04/pier-luigi-nervi-system/), [Princeton CASCE: Nervi and the Italian tradition of ribbed vaults](https://casce.princeton.edu/node/85), [Tor Vergata record](https://art.torvergata.it/handle/2108/215957)

### Block Research Group / ETH Zurich: HiLo slab, Empa NEST (2020) and Rippmann Floor System
- **What:**
  - A funicular ribbed concrete floor cast in place over 43 small 3D-printed formwork parts (binder-jet sand) for the bottom.
  - CNC-cut polyurethane foam blocks were used as formwork between the ribs and **stay in place as insulation**.
  - Commercialised as the Rippmann Floor System by the ETH spin-off VAULTED, which claims up to 70% less concrete and 80% less steel (company figures).
- **Takeaway:** printed formwork for ribs, plus formwork that stays as insulation between the ribs. That is our iteration 2 (trays + insulation).
- **Sources:** [ETH research collection: ribbed floor slabs with 3D-printed formwork](https://www.research-collection.ethz.ch/entities/publication/fc511e49-ad57-4238-a835-14b9db93e1cd), [DETAIL: HiRes concrete slab, NEST](https://detail.de/de_en/hires-concrete-slab-im-empa-nest-dubendorf), [VAULTED commercial project](https://Startupticker.ch/en/news/vaulted-to-build-first-large-commercial-project), [Met Museum: Rippmann floor](https://www.metmuseum.org/zh/perspectives/rippman-floor-system-from-concept-to-construction)

## 2. 3D-printed concrete in compression

### Striatus bridge, Venice (2021): Block Research Group (ETH) + Zaha Hadid Architects CODE + incremental3D + Holcim
- **What:**
  - A 16 × 12 m arched footbridge of **53 3D-printed concrete blocks**, held by compression only: no reinforcement, mortar or glue.
  - Steel ties take the thrust, and neoprene pads sit between the blocks.
  - **Print layers run orthogonal to the compression flow**, printed by a 6-axis robot.
  - It was dry assembled, so it can be taken apart and reused.
- **Takeaway:**
  - print direction perpendicular to the force;
  - a compression-only form built from discrete printed pieces;
  - design for disassembly.
- **Not found:** details of its temporary support (falsework). See striatusbridge.com or BRG papers.
- **Sources:** [Holcim](https://www.holcim.com/striatus-bridge-venice-biennale-2021-inauguration), [Architect Magazine](https://www.architectmagazine.com/technology/worlds-first-3d-printed-concrete-arched-bridge-completed_o), [Architizer](https://www.architizer.com/projects/striatus-3d-printed-bridge)

### TECLA, Massa Lombarda, Italy (2021): Mario Cucinella Architects + WASP
- **What:**
  - Two clay domes 3D-printed on site, with **no support structure during construction**. The dome shape encloses the building without formwork.
  - 350 layers, printed by two synchronised Crane WASP printers, in about 200 hours.
  - Local riverbed clay was used, with rice husk fibres. The undulating walls also act as thermal mass.
- **Takeaway:** a dome geometry printed in place without scaffolding, using local material. Its durability over decades in wet climates is not yet proven, which matters for Ishigaki.
- **Sources:** [New Atlas](https://newatlas.com/3d-printing/tecla-wasp-mario-cucinella-clay), [Designboom](https://www.designboom.com/architecture/tecla-mario-cucinella-3d-printed-home-clay-interiors-04-15-2021/), [Studio Matrx](https://www.studiomatrx.org/guides/tecla-3d-printed-house)

### Armadillo Vault, Venice Biennale (2016): BRG + Ochsendorf DeJong & Block + Escobedo Group
- **What:**
  - **399 limestone voussoirs**, no mortar, a 16 m span, minimum thickness about 5 cm, with tension ties.
  - Assembled over a standard scaffold that was removed when the last stone was set.
  - Pre-assembled in Texas, then reassembled in Venice in just over two weeks.
- **Takeaway:**
  - it shows how many discrete pieces a funicular vault can have;
  - it needed temporary scaffold until the last piece was placed, the "a compression shell only stands when complete" problem.
- **Sources:** [Domus](https://domusweb.it/en/news/2016/10/06/armadillo_vault.html), [Stone-ideas](https://www.stone-ideas.com/53092/the-armadillo-vault-at-this-years-venice-biennale-hovering-stone-puzzle-comprising-399-elements-and-a-mass-of-24-t/), [Designing Buildings](https://designingbuildings.co.uk/wiki/Beyond%20bending%20-%20The%20Armadillo%20Vault)

## 3. Minimal scaffolding / no centering

### Brunelleschi: dome of Florence Cathedral (1420–36)
- **What:**
  - Built **without wooden centering**.
  - Herringbone brick courses stopped bricks sliding inward before the mortar set, and passed the weight of fresh courses to the ribs.
  - It is a double shell with ribs, and internal chains of iron and stone contain the thrust.
  - Each course held itself as the dome rose.
- **Takeaway:** ring-by-ring construction where each closed course is self-supporting, plus interlocking pieces and a ribbed skeleton. That is our "build ring by ring, prop only the ring in progress".
- **Sources:** [Museo Galileo: the dome structure](https://brunelleschi.imss.fi.it/itineraries/multimedia/TheDomeStructure.html), [Secrets of the Duomo](https://bacweb.org/es/node/432)

### Rafael Guastavino: thin tile (timbrel) vaults (late 19th–early 20th c.)
- **What:**
  - Thin tiles bonded with **fast-setting plaster of Paris**, so the vault needs little or no support from below. More tile layers in Portland cement mortar are added on top.
  - Vaults are built out from a wall in successive arcs. One 135 ft dome was built from above without supporting scaffolding.
  - "No formwork" is best read as "minimal formwork": an MIT replica still used a light timber frame.
- **Takeaway:** fast-setting material makes each new piece self-supporting quickly. This is our "accelerator in the ribs so each ring stands in hours".
- **Sources:** [Traditional Building: Guastavino thin tile vaults](https://www.traditionalbuilding.com/features/guastavino-thin-tile-vaults), [STRUCTURE magazine](https://structuremag.org/?p=2046), [MIT CEE replica](https://cee.mit.edu/cee-students-build-replica-of-guastavino-vaulted-ceiling-at-boston-public-library-2)

### Nubian vault (traditional, Egypt / Sahel)
- **What:**
  - A mud-brick catenary vault built **without centering or formwork**. The first bricks lean on an inward-leaning gable wall, each course leans on the previous one, and mud mortar holds them until the curve closes.
  - The method has limits: very flat shapes can't be built with these courses.
- **Takeaway:** leaning courses on the part already built. This applies to our steep lower rings (bars 30–45° from vertical).
- **Sources:** [Wikipedia: Nubian vault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nubian_vault), [ArchDaily](https://www.archdaily.com/1031878/the-nubian-vault-reviving-ancient-techniques-for-modern-solutions), [Auroville Earth Institute: AVD construction](https://dev.earth-auroville.com/avd-construction/)

### Eladio Dieste: reinforced brick vaults, Uruguay (e.g. Church of Atlántida, 1958–60)
- **What:**
  - Thin brick shells laid on **reusable, movable formwork** that moved along as construction progressed, without permanent scaffolding.
  - At Atlántida the roof was built in 6 m portions on one movable form.
  - The forms could be struck about a day after laying.
  - Dieste designed his own machines, including motorised systems to raise and lower the formwork.
- **Takeaway:** **one small movable support reused many times**. That is our "build one 1/8 sector, move the props around 8 times" and the "moving band of props".
- **Sources:** [Wikipedia: Eladio Dieste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eladio_Dieste), [LACGEO: Church of Atlántida](https://lacgeo.com/node/759), [Architectural League: the work of Eladio Dieste](https://archleague.org/?p=39714), [Pedreschi paper (Cambridge)](https://www.arct.cam.ac.uk/system/files/documents/vol-3-2451-2468-pedreschi.pdf)

### Droneport prototype, Venice Biennale (2016): Norman Foster Foundation, with Ochsendorf DeJong & Block and BRG
- **What:**
  - A full-scale vaulted masonry bay of compressed-earth bricks (Durabric) with a tile layer, using Guastavino techniques.
  - Built in three weeks by six people.
- **Sources disagree on formwork:** Cambridge says the design can be built without formwork, while Foster/Archilovers describe a kit that includes basic formwork. Cite with care.
- **Takeaway:** a fast, low-tech vault built by a small crew with local material, designed for minimal formwork.
- **Sources:** [Cambridge Engineering](https://www.eng.cam.ac.uk/node/3497), [MIT Spectrum: From the vault](https://betterworld.mit.edu/spectrum/issues/fall-2016/from-the-vault), [Norman Foster Foundation](https://normanfosterfoundation.org/project/droneport/), [Archilovers](https://archilovers.com/projects/184300/the-norman-foster-foundation-the-droneport-prototype.html)

## 4. Graded thickness: heavier low, lighter high

### Pantheon, Rome (c. 125 AD)
- **What:**
  - The dome thins from bottom to top. The figures most often given are about 6.4 m at the base and about 1.2 m at the oculus; sources vary and none found here was scholarly.
  - The aggregate changes with height: basalt (dense) at the base, tuff midway, **pumice** (light) near the oculus.
- **Takeaway:** mass low and lightness high. This is our grading: solid blocks in the lowest rings, thick trays in the middle, the thinnest trays near the crown.
- **Sources:** [Voicemap: anatomy of the dome](https://voicemap.me/tour/rome/the-pantheon-the-mysteries-and-secrets-of-the-world-s-largest-dome/sites/the-anatomy-of-the-dome), [edatlas Pantheon PDF](https://www.edatlas.it/Scarica/arte/clil/pantheon/assets/pantheon.pdf), [Buffalo Architecture and History: Pantheon](https://buffaloah.com/a/virtual/italy/rome/pan/int/index.html)

## How they map onto our construction sequence

| Step in our sequence | Precedent(s) |
|---|---|
| Printed pieces carry half the rib; ribs cast in situ with rebar | Nervi Turin Hall B; HiLo / Rippmann floor |
| Thin trays as permanent formwork, insulation left between ribs | HiLo (foam left as insulation); Nervi ferrocement |
| Print layers orthogonal to the force | Striatus |
| Light, discontinuous scaffold instead of full falsework | Nervi Palazzetto |
| Ring-by-ring, each closed ring self-supporting | Brunelleschi |
| Steep lower rings lean on what is built | Nubian vault |
| Fast set so each ring stands quickly | Guastavino (plaster of Paris) |
| One movable support reused around the dome | Dieste (movable formwork) |
| Thicker/heavier low, thinner/lighter high | Pantheon |
| Dome printed/built with no support at all (limit case) | TECLA; Droneport (disputed) |
